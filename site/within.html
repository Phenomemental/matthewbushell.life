Within

In Northampton, on UK time.

Every night in my book opens the same way. The hour, the body, what’s in the room. It’s 21:07. Very little pain today. Haven is unsettled beside me. Dr Louie Gardiner’s Presence in Action taught me to do that on purpose. What are the facts? What are the feelings? What are the fictions? What am I here for? Ask, and the next thing changes.

A place is in me as much as I am in it. I learned that from Alan Rayner, in fourteen years of letters. Water shapes the land and is shaped by it. I’ve carried a sentence for years, and I’ve said it in rooms: Nature herself never excludes you. Only some people, in some places do.

The drawing on the first page has within at its centre, and home, work, community and cyberspace spiralling out from it. Ray Oldenburg gave me the first three, 5th Place the fourth. The fifth place is all of them at once, each in the other: being who we are, whichever room we’re in. Within is where that gets held. It starts here, where it all gets lived, and it isn’t a retreat from the other four: it’s the one thing I take into all of them. What follows is the practice, in the order 5th Place taught me: awareness, acceptance, action.

Awareness, acceptance, action is 5th Place’s own framework for change. This page keeps its order.

Four questions

Facts, feelings, fictions, purpose. Presence in Action is Dr Louie Gardiner’s practice, and the whole of it fits in one noticing: which of the four am I in right now? Notice, and what happens next changes.

Acceptance

Welcoming what’s there, all of it, without first needing the story.

Shape of Emotion

Matthew Green and Chantal Dawtrey’s four-step method for the feeling itself: find it in the body, give it a shape, and work with that, without first needing the story behind it. I first learned to ask what shape a feeling is at my own dining table.

Wholeless

A word I made, for a way of living that didn’t have one. People hear it as wholeness, or as something missing, and it’s neither: -⁠less as in boundless. One axis, two ends. The ends are not edges, and there’s nothing outside. I don’t live between mania and depression. I live in both, each in the other, and nothing has to be cut away for me to be me. It’s new, and the book is where it’s being worked out.

Natural Inclusion

A way of seeing I learned from Alan Rayner: each in the other, nothing with a hard edge. Not from a book. From letters: fourteen years with Alan, and the circle of companions around him that I found while I was at university, scientists, artists, writers, friends, who gather online and write to each other about love, healing, beauty and how nature actually works. Two hundred letters in the last five years alone, some of my purest writing. Roy and Larry have died; the circle keeps them. Whitehead and Hartshorne came by way of Adam Blatner, the only psychiatrist I met as a friend. The circle is where I think.

Action

What I do with a feeling, every day, and how a day gets kept.

Five pillars

Emotional fitness is what you can do with a feeling, every day: your capacity to regulate how you feel. I learned it with Matthew Green and Chantal Dawtrey at 5th Place, and now I teach it. Thinking, because thoughts shape how you feel. Feeling, and welcoming all of them, without judgement. Moving, so body and mind stay together. Nourishing: food, sleep, creativity, care. Relating: people, and a community to belong to. Something to do with a feeling, every day: it’s the real essence of this story.

Six portals

A day can be told six ways: what happened, what’s ahead, what you made of it, a choice, what matters, what you felt. Presence is the seventh, at the centre. I call them P6, and I keep my own days through them. If I notice I’m all fictions, I ask myself for the facts. If I’m all outcomes, I ask what matters. Sundial, the app on my phone, is built on the portals, Shape of Emotion and the pillars: you talk, it keeps your day with you, and the dial follows the real sun.

Some words

Wholeless
My word. Living in both ends of one axis, each in the other, with nothing cut away. -⁠less as in boundless.

Restorying
Telling a memory again, and differently, so it survives. The belief under the book: we recover by restorying.

Home, work, community, cyberspace, and all of them together: being who we are. Also 5th Place, my friends’ practice, where I trained.
Natural inclusion
Alan Rayner’s way of seeing: nothing has a hard edge, and each thing is in the other, as a place is in me and I am in it.
