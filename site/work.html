The second place

Work

What I do with people, and how I got here.

My working life began at seventeen and a half, in the Royal Signals: encryption keys and secure links, and a Leadership Award for Best Recruit. Technology became my trade. For most of twenty years I designed networks, trained the people who used them and advised the people who ran them. Outwardly fine.

The other road began at university. I arrived at twenty-six to study creative writing and found the applied arts and health movement, people who believed art could help people heal. I took the arts out into the town with ArtBlast, and worked part-time as a rehabilitation assistant with people living with brain injury. When I wrote to Adam Blatner, he pointed me towards the caring professions, a road I was already on. At St Andrew’s I worked on the brain injury wards, then as a veterans’ mental health advocate, back among soldiers, on the other side of the desk. In 2015 I began facilitating the Bipolar UK peer support group in Northampton.

In 2016 I struggled to find my feet and went back to IT. In October 2021, deeply depressed, I left it for the second time, and this time I didn’t go back. My first real path was with Petra Velzeboer, a psychotherapist who came through addiction and poor mental health herself and built PVL, a consultancy helping workplaces become places where people can thrive. I trained and spoke with her, and learned that my own story could come into a professional room and still be mine. Matthew Green and Chantal Dawtrey were both coaches, and I wanted to be a coach. It turned out I had to become a coach to myself first, something I had completely overlooked in my ambition to help others. Then therapeutic coaching, 5th Place and the Recovery College, and now my main job: supporting lived experience recovery organisations across North Northamptonshire. The thread through all of it is the same, sitting with people who are where I’ve been.

LERO Coordinator

The Forward Trust, North Northamptonshire, since July 2026

My main job, thirty hours a week. A Lived Experience Recovery Organisation is recovery run by the people who’ve lived it, and I support their development across the county. The people who come forward are experts by experience, and the culture gets set in how we treat each other. The Forward Trust works with addiction and criminal justice. I’m learning how a LERO is built on ImROC’s mentoring programme, with the Chase Recovery team in North Wales.

Therapeutic Coach

Since April 2022, and UK and Europe representative for 5th Place

One to one since April 2022 with people living with bipolar: patterns, stability, what to do with a feeling on an ordinary day. Therapeutic coaching brings counselling and coaching together, so we can look back at what happened as well as forward to what’s next. Frances Masters trained me in it, at the Integrated Coaching Academy, in her Fusion model. I’m an emotional fitness practitioner too, trained by Matthew Green and Chantal Dawtrey, and their representative for the UK and Europe, taking Shape of Emotion and the Emotional Fitness Model to organisations, schools and communities: in 2025, a primary school in Soweto and a room of social workers in Johannesburg. I’m writing a chapter for their book, Shape of Emotion. Sometimes I use digital tools with the people I coach, to help them develop their own therapeutic stories.

Recovery College Peer Trainer

Northamptonshire Healthcare NHS Foundation Trust, since February 2026

I run Living with Bipolar, for people living with bipolar and those around them, with more dates being published soon. I’d already co-delivered it here before I joined. I also co-facilitate other courses and workshops, and co-write what gets taught, on the principle that services are best designed and delivered by people who’ve used them.
