Five places

Home, work, community, cyberspace, each in the other.

I live on a wide street in Northampton. More sky than bricks. If you stand outside and look left, you can watch the sun rise above the tree line on the other side of the valley. Walk far enough and the street gives way to mud, and the mud to water, and the noise in my head goes quiet.

A place is in me as much as I am in it. I learned that from Alan Rayner, in fourteen years of letters. Water shapes the land and is shaped by it. I’ve carried a sentence for years, and I’ve said it in rooms: Nature herself never excludes you. Only some people, in some places do.

It starts within. Ray Oldenburg, in The Great Good Place, wrote about home, work and the third place, where community happens. My friends Matthew Green and Chantal Dawtrey at 5th Place kept counting. Cyberspace is the fourth. The fifth is all of it, each in the other, being who we are.

Cyber-psychology

At university, studying creative writing, I got curious about cyber-psychology. My dissertation was called The Spirit of the Internet Guru: a man in a room, exchanging messages with an avatar, never sure who or what was on the other end. That was 2011. Now I spend my evenings in a room, exchanging messages with AIs. I write about it in the book.
