The third place

Community

Recovery is something we do with each other.

The first time I sat in a room of people who understood, at the Bipolar UK group in Northampton in 2015, it changed what I thought was possible. For years I’d been treated one to one, as a problem to be solved. In that room nobody was fixing anybody. Ray Oldenburg wrote that a third place is a leveller: inside it you’re accepted for who you are. That room was one. Peer support moved me from what’s wrong with you to what happened to you, and I’ve been in rooms like it ever since: facilitating that group, a podcast and a peer community of close to a thousand people online, Action for Happiness, and now lived experience recovery organisations across the county. When things went badly for me again, it was people who held me up and let me be human. That’s what I mean by community: people organising themselves around what they already have, and around each other. This September I walked with peers from Community Recovery at the UK Recovery Walk in Bradford, talking about recovery openly, out in public. I believe that inclusive social movements powered by lived experience will respectfully challenge age-old systems and practices for the better. The greatest dream, for me, is to rise together.

Lived Experience Recovery Organisations

Community Recovery, and LEROs across North Northamptonshire

A Lived Experience Recovery Organisation is recovery run by the people who’ve lived it. Recovery is a narrower word than the life it names: in drug and alcohol services it shrinks to one substance or one behaviour, and the isolation that was there before the substance doesn’t leave with it. A LERO takes the whole person: addiction, criminal justice, homelessness and mental health in the same room, because that’s how they come, in one person. What makes a LERO is who holds it: people with lived experience, organising themselves to support the recovery of others, whatever its shape, constituted or not, funded or not. What we’re doing is growing communities. Supporting LEROs across the county is my job, for The Forward Trust. I’m also a member and volunteer of Community Recovery, a distinct organisation here with its own leaders. In Chiba I spoke on bipolar and substance use. It’s ground I know.

Expert by Experience

West and North Northamptonshire Councils, since April 2026

Co-production. Recovery happens with each other, so the people who use a service belong in the room where it’s designed, with lived experience leading where it can. Adult social care asks people with lived experience in to help shape its services, and I’m one of them. I’ve worked the same way with Northamptonshire Mind, Mental Health Northants Collaboration and Voluntary Impact Northamptonshire.

Lived Experience Council

International Society for Bipolar Disorders, since November 2025

People who live with bipolar, working with researchers and clinicians around the world on education, research and clinical care. It followed Chiba, where Dr Andrea Vassilev gave me the chance to apply and I spoke about bipolar and substance use.

Peer support

Intentional Peer Support practitioner, ImROC Peer Support Worker

It moved me from what’s wrong with you to what happened to you. More a relationship than a service. Trained in Intentional Peer Support and ImROC, with my own Wellness Recovery Action Plan. What lingers is the feeling of sitting in a room with people who share a quiet, intuitive understanding.
