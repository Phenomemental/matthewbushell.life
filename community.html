Recovery is something we do with each other.

LERO Coordinator

The Forward Trust, North Northamptonshire, since July 2026

A Lived Experience Recovery Organisation is recovery run by the people who’ve lived it. I support their development across the county. The people who come forward are experts by experience, and the culture gets set in how we treat each other. I’m also on ImROC’s LERO mentoring programme, and a member and volunteer of Community Recovery, a distinct organisation here with its own leaders. The Forward Trust works with addiction and criminal justice, and recovery here means addiction, criminal justice, homelessness and mental health in the same room, because that’s how they come, in one person. In Chiba I spoke on bipolar and substance use. It’s ground I know.

What I keep saying

Recovery is a narrower word than the life it names. In drug and alcohol services it shrinks to one substance or one behaviour, and the isolation that was there before the substance doesn’t leave with it. A recovery organisation run by people who’ve lived it takes the whole person, because that’s who turns up.

We say we’re building organisations when what we’re doing is growing communities. What makes a LERO isn’t its shape, constituted or not, funded or not. It’s who holds it: people with lived experience, organising themselves to support the recovery of others.

Men’s mental health, suicide, stigma. The silence I was first diagnosed into is still out there, and men are still dying in it. I say so wherever I’m given a microphone.

We are neither broken nor fixed; we’re in motion. Recovery doesn’t come from interventions delivered at us. It happens with each other, so the people who use a service belong in the room where it’s designed. Co-production, not consultation, and lived experience leading where it can.

What I write here is my own, and doesn’t speak for any organisation I work with.
